Landing page vs website for a small business: which one do you need?

2026-10-26

If you run a local business and are about to get online, you will hit this question fast: do I need a landing page or a full website? Agencies often blur the two, which makes the choice feel harder than it is. This guide explains the difference in plain terms and gives you a simple way to decide.

What is a landing page?

A landing page is a single page built around one goal: getting a visitor to take one action, such as calling you, requesting a quote or booking an appointment. It usually contains a clear headline, a short description of your services, a few reviews, your hours and location, and a prominent button or phone number.

There is no maze of menus. A visitor arrives, understands what you do within seconds, and either contacts you or leaves. That focus is the whole point.

What is a website?

A website is a set of connected pages: typically a home page, services, about, gallery or portfolio, contact, and often a blog. It gives visitors more room to explore and gives search engines more content to index. It also takes more time to plan, write and maintain.

The key differences

| | Landing page | Small website (4–6 pages) | |---|---|---| | Goal | One action (call, quote, book) | Inform, build trust, rank for several searches | | Build time | Days | 1–3 weeks | | Typical cost | $400–$1,200 | $1,000–$3,500 | | Content needed | Short and focused | Several pages of copy and photos | | Search visibility | Limited to a few terms | Broader, grows over time | | Maintenance | Minimal | Moderate |

When a landing page is enough

A landing page is usually the right start if:

  • You offer a small number of services that are easy to explain (a plumber, a barber, a cleaning service).
  • Most of your customers come from referrals, Google Business Profile or social media, and you need a trustworthy place to send them.
  • You want to be online this week, not next quarter.
  • Your budget is tight and you want to test whether the website brings in calls before investing more.

A concrete example: a family-run plumbing company in Phoenix gets most jobs by phone. A one-page site with "24/7 emergency plumber in Phoenix", a tap-to-call button, service areas, three customer reviews and a license number answers every question a worried homeowner has. Nothing more is needed to turn a search into a call.

When you need a full website

Step up to a multi-page site if:

  • You have many distinct services, each of which people search for separately (for example a dental practice with orthodontics, implants and emergency care).
  • You want to rank on Google for several keywords or neighborhoods.
  • You need to show a portfolio, a menu, pricing tables or team profiles.
  • You plan to publish regular content, such as a blog, to attract customers over time.

More pages mean more chances to appear in search results, and more space to build the trust that higher-priced services require.

A practical way to decide

Ask yourself three questions:

  1. Can I explain what I do in one screen? If yes, a landing page can carry it.
  2. Do customers search for several different services? If yes, you will want separate pages.
  3. Is my main goal calls this month or authority over years? Calls now point to a landing page; authority points to a website.

You do not have to choose forever. Many businesses launch a landing page first, learn what customers ask about, and then expand into additional pages. A good landing page is not wasted work: it becomes the home page of the larger site.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Paying for pages nobody needs. An "About our philosophy" page rarely wins a customer; a clear phone number does.
  • Cramming a landing page with everything. If it has six different calls to action, it has none.
  • Skipping the basics. Whichever you choose, it must load fast on a phone, show your address and hours, and make contacting you effortless.
  • Forgetting upkeep. Outdated hours or a broken form cost you real customers.

FAQ

Is a landing page enough for a small business?

For many local businesses, yes, especially at the start. If you have a few simple services and get customers through calls, referrals or your Google Business Profile, a well-made landing page can do most of the work of a website at a lower cost.

Can a landing page rank on Google?

It can rank for a small number of closely related searches, particularly local ones like "plumber in Phoenix". It will not rank for many different topics the way a multi-page site can. If search traffic is central to your plan, a larger website is the better foundation. No one can honestly guarantee rankings either way.

Can I start with a landing page and add pages later?

Yes, and it is often the smartest route. Build the landing page now, watch which questions customers ask, then add service pages or a blog where there is demand. Pick a builder and structure that lets you grow without starting over.
