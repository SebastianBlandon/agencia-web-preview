14:T1615,<p>If you run a local business and are about to get online, you will hit this question fast: do I need a landing page or a full website? Agencies often blur the two, which makes the choice feel harder than it is. This guide explains the difference in plain terms and gives you a simple way to decide.</p>
<h2>What is a landing page?</h2>
<p>A landing page is a single page built around one goal: getting a visitor to take one action, such as calling you, requesting a quote or booking an appointment. It usually contains a clear headline, a short description of your services, a few reviews, your hours and location, and a prominent button or phone number.</p>
<p>There is no maze of menus. A visitor arrives, understands what you do within seconds, and either contacts you or leaves. That focus is the whole point.</p>
<h2>What is a website?</h2>
<p>A website is a set of connected pages: typically a home page, services, about, gallery or portfolio, contact, and often a blog. It gives visitors more room to explore and gives search engines more content to index. It also takes more time to plan, write and maintain.</p>
<h2>The key differences</h2>
<p>| | Landing page | Small website (4–6 pages) |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | One action (call, quote, book) | Inform, build trust, rank for several searches |
| Build time | Days | 1–3 weeks |
| Typical cost | $400–$1,200 | $1,000–$3,500 |
| Content needed | Short and focused | Several pages of copy and photos |
| Search visibility | Limited to a few terms | Broader, grows over time |
| Maintenance | Minimal | Moderate |</p>
<h2>When a landing page is enough</h2>
<p>A landing page is usually the right start if:</p>
<ul>
<li>You offer a small number of services that are easy to explain (a plumber, a barber, a cleaning service).</li>
<li>Most of your customers come from referrals, Google Business Profile or social media, and you need a trustworthy place to send them.</li>
<li>You want to be online this week, not next quarter.</li>
<li>Your budget is tight and you want to test whether the website brings in calls before investing more.</li>
</ul>
<p>A concrete example: a family-run plumbing company in Phoenix gets most jobs by phone. A one-page site with "24/7 emergency plumber in Phoenix", a tap-to-call button, service areas, three customer reviews and a license number answers every question a worried homeowner has. Nothing more is needed to turn a search into a call.</p>
<h2>When you need a full website</h2>
<p>Step up to a multi-page site if:</p>
<ul>
<li>You have many distinct services, each of which people search for separately (for example a dental practice with orthodontics, implants and emergency care).</li>
<li>You want to rank on Google for several keywords or neighborhoods.</li>
<li>You need to show a portfolio, a menu, pricing tables or team profiles.</li>
<li>You plan to publish regular content, such as a blog, to attract customers over time.</li>
</ul>
<p>More pages mean more chances to appear in search results, and more space to build the trust that higher-priced services require.</p>
<h2>A practical way to decide</h2>
<p>Ask yourself three questions:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Can I explain what I do in one screen?</strong> If yes, a landing page can carry it.</li>
<li><strong>Do customers search for several different services?</strong> If yes, you will want separate pages.</li>
<li><strong>Is my main goal calls this month or authority over years?</strong> Calls now point to a landing page; authority points to a website.</li>
</ol>
<p>You do not have to choose forever. Many businesses launch a landing page first, learn what customers ask about, and then expand into additional pages. A good landing page is not wasted work: it becomes the home page of the larger site.</p>
<h2>Common mistakes to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Paying for pages nobody needs.</strong> An "About our philosophy" page rarely wins a customer; a clear phone number does.</li>
<li><strong>Cramming a landing page with everything.</strong> If it has six different calls to action, it has none.</li>
<li><strong>Skipping the basics.</strong> Whichever you choose, it must load fast on a phone, show your address and hours, and make contacting you effortless.</li>
<li><strong>Forgetting upkeep.</strong> Outdated hours or a broken form cost you real customers.</li>
</ul>
<p>For a deeper look at budgeting, see our guide on <a href="/agencia-web-preview/blog/how-much-does-a-small-business-website-cost/">how much a small business website costs</a>. If you want to compare options for your business, take a look at our <a href="/agencia-web-preview/services/">services</a>.</p>
<h2>FAQ</h2>
<h3>Is a landing page enough for a small business?</h3>
<p>For many local businesses, yes, especially at the start. If you have a few simple services and get customers through calls, referrals or your Google Business Profile, a well-made landing page can do most of the work of a website at a lower cost.</p>
<h3>Can a landing page rank on Google?</h3>
<p>It can rank for a small number of closely related searches, particularly local ones like "plumber in Phoenix". It will not rank for many different topics the way a multi-page site can. If search traffic is central to your plan, a larger website is the better foundation. No one can honestly guarantee rankings either way.</p>
<h3>Can I start with a landing page and add pages later?</h3>
<p>Yes, and it is often the smartest route. Build the landing page now, watch which questions customers ask, then add service pages or a blog where there is demand. Pick a builder and structure that lets you grow without starting over.</p>
