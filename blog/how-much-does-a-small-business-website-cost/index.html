How much does a small business website cost in 2026?

2026-10-19

If you search for "how much does a small business website cost," you will find answers from $200 to $50,000. Both ends are technically true, and neither is useful for a plumber, a dentist or a family restaurant. This guide gives you realistic ranges for a local business, explains what moves the price, and shows how to decide what you actually need.

The short answer

For a local business, expect to pay:

  • DIY builder (Wix, Squarespace, etc.): $15–$45 per month, plus your own time. Cheap in cash, expensive in hours.
  • One-page landing page from a small agency or freelancer: $400–$1,200 one time.
  • Small website of 4–6 pages: $1,000–$3,500 one time.
  • Custom or e-commerce site: $4,000 and up, often much more.
  • Ongoing costs: a monthly amount for hosting, domain renewal, backups and small edits.

Large agencies quoting $10,000+ are usually selling strategy, branding and custom features that a neighborhood business rarely needs.

What drives the price

Number of pages

Every page needs copy, photos, layout and testing. A landing page that says who you are, what you do, shows reviews and gives a clear way to call or message you is often enough to start getting customers. A 5-page site (home, services, about, gallery, contact) costs more because there is simply more to build and write.

Who writes the content

If you provide finished text and photos, the price drops. If the agency has to research your business, write the copy and source images, the price rises. Ask which one is included.

Features

Booking forms, online payments, multilingual versions and member areas each add real work. A contact button that opens WhatsApp or a phone call adds almost nothing.

Ownership and hosting

Some cheap offers lock your site inside their platform, so you pay monthly forever and cannot leave. Ask whether you own the files and the domain.

A concrete example

Take a dental clinic with three dentists and no website, only a Facebook page. What it needs: a clear headline with the city, services (cleanings, whitening, implants), real patient reviews, hours, a map and an appointment button. That is a single-page landing site. At typical small-agency rates, a site like this usually costs a few hundred dollars to build, plus a modest monthly fee if you want hosting, domain and small edits handled for you. Adding separate pages per treatment, a blog and online booking pushes the price higher.

Where the money goes

A fair quote usually splits into four parts. Planning and structure decide what goes on the page and in what order. Design and build turn that into a fast, mobile-friendly layout. Copy and images make the page persuasive instead of generic. Finally, launch work covers the domain connection, analytics, a basic Google Business Profile check and testing on real phones. When a quote does not show these parts, ask what is included, because the cheapest offers often skip the copy and the launch work, which are the parts that actually bring in customers.

One-time cost versus total cost of ownership

Compare options over three years, not over the first month. A do-it-yourself builder plan costs a monthly fee for as long as you use it, and you still do the work yourself. A professionally built landing page plus a maintenance plan costs more, but the pages are written for you, edits are handled, and you keep control of your domain. The right choice depends on whether your time is worth more than the difference. For most owners who run a shop, a clinic or a crew, it is.

Hidden costs to ask about

  • Domain renewal (usually $12–$20 per year).
  • Charges per revision after the first round.
  • Stock photos or fonts billed separately.
  • Plugin or app subscriptions on builder platforms.
  • Fees to move your site if you leave.

How to choose without overspending

  1. Start with the smallest site that lets a customer find you, trust you and contact you.
  2. Pay for speed and clarity, not animations.
  3. Insist on mobile-first design: most local searches happen on a phone.
  4. Get the price, delivery date and number of revisions in writing.
  5. Add pages later, when real customers ask questions your site does not answer.

FAQ

Is a free website builder good enough? It can work for a hobby or a temporary page. For a business that depends on local search, builder sites tend to be slower, harder to customize for SEO and tied to a monthly fee for as long as you use them.

How long does it take to build a small business website? A landing page typically takes about a week once the texts and photos are ready. A multi-page site usually takes two to four weeks, depending on how quickly feedback comes back.

Do I have to pay every month? The build itself can be a one-time cost. Hosting, the domain and updates are ongoing, but they should be small and clearly listed. Be wary of any plan that does not let you keep your domain or export your site.
