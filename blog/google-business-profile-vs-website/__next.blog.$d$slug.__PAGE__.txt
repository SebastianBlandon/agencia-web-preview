2:T1795,<p>If you run a local business, you have probably heard two pieces of advice that seem to compete: "claim your Google Business Profile" and "get a website." The Google Business Profile vs website question comes up constantly, usually from owners who want to spend as little as possible. The short answer is that they do different jobs, and the best setup uses both. Here is how to think about each one and how to decide where to start.</p>
<h2>What a Google Business Profile does</h2>
<p>A Google Business Profile (GBP) is the free listing that appears in Google Maps and in the local panel on the right of search results. It shows your name, address, hours, phone number, photos, reviews and a few buttons such as "Call" and "Directions."</p>
<p>It is excellent at one thing: being found by people who are already nearby and searching for what you do. Someone types "plumber near me" on their phone, sees three businesses on the map, and taps one. That moment happens on Google, not on your site.</p>
<p>Strengths of a profile:</p>
<ul>
<li>It is free and quick to set up (verification can take a few days).</li>
<li>Reviews and photos build trust right inside the search results.</li>
<li>It gives one-tap calling and directions.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Where a profile falls short</h2>
<p>A profile is a rented space. Google controls the layout, the fields, what is shown and what is hidden. You can't tell your story, explain your process, show a full price range or build a page for each service. You also can't fully control what appears: suggested edits from users, competitor ads on your listing and changes to Google's design can all affect you.</p>
<p>Other limits to keep in mind:</p>
<ul>
<li>It is hard to explain what makes you different in a few lines of text.</li>
<li>You can't collect leads with your own form or track where they came from in any detail.</li>
<li>If your listing is suspended or flagged, you may lose your main source of calls overnight, with nothing to fall back on.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What a website does that a profile can't</h2>
<p>A website is the one place online that you own. It can answer the questions a customer has before they call: what do you do, in which areas, roughly how much, how does it work and why should I pick you over the next business.</p>
<p>A site also helps you in ways that are easy to miss:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>It confirms you are real.</strong> Many customers check the website before calling, especially for larger jobs.</li>
<li><strong>It targets more searches.</strong> A profile mostly appears for "near me" searches. A site with a page per service can appear for "water heater replacement in Austin" and similar specific queries.</li>
<li><strong>It supports your profile.</strong> Google uses your website as one signal when deciding how to rank your listing, so the two reinforce each other.</li>
<li><strong>It works for every channel.</strong> You can link it from business cards, social profiles, invoices and ads.</li>
</ul>
<p>For a closer look at what a small site includes, see our <a href="/agencia-web-preview/services/">services</a> page.</p>
<h2>A simple example</h2>
<p>Imagine a small bakery with a well-kept Google profile: 80 reviews, nice photos, correct hours. It gets calls from people nearby. But when a customer wants a wedding cake, they look for custom order details, flavors, lead times and starting prices. The profile has none of that, so the customer either guesses or goes to a competitor whose website answers everything. A single landing page with that information turns a browser into an order.</p>
<h2>How to decide what to set up first</h2>
<p>Use this order if you are starting from zero:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Claim and complete your Google Business Profile.</strong> It is free, fast and gives immediate visibility to nearby customers. Add accurate hours, categories, services and real photos.</li>
<li><strong>Ask happy customers for reviews.</strong> Do it politely and consistently, without incentives that break Google's rules.</li>
<li><strong>Build a simple website.</strong> One focused landing page is enough to start: what you do, where, proof, and a clear way to contact you. You can add pages later.</li>
<li><strong>Link them together.</strong> Put your website on the profile, and your profile reviews or map on the site.</li>
</ol>
<p>If your budget is tight, the profile comes first. But treat it as a first step, not a replacement. For the price side of that decision, read <a href="/agencia-web-preview/blog/how-much-does-a-small-business-website-cost/">how much a small business website costs</a>.</p>
<h2>Common mistakes to avoid</h2>
<ul>
<li>Leaving the website field of your profile empty or pointing it at a social page.</li>
<li>Using different business names, phone numbers or addresses across the profile and site. Keep them identical.</li>
<li>Letting reviews go unanswered. A short, polite reply helps future customers.</li>
<li>Assuming either tool brings results instantly. Both take consistent work, and nobody can promise a specific ranking.</li>
</ul>
<h2>FAQ</h2>
<h3>Can I just use a Google Business Profile instead of a website?</h3>
<p>You can, and many businesses start that way. It works best for simple, walk-in or call-in services. But you will be limited in explaining your offer, you will depend entirely on Google's platform, and you will miss searches that a dedicated site could capture.</p>
<h3>Does having a website help my Google Business Profile rank?</h3>
<p>A website is one of several signals Google uses to understand your business, along with reviews, proximity and profile completeness. A clear, accurate site that matches your profile details can help, but no one can guarantee a specific position.</p>
<h3>Which is better for a new local business?</h3>
<p>Start with the profile because it is free and quick, then add a simple one-page website as soon as you can. Together they cover both the "near me" searches and the customers who want to learn more before they contact you.</p>
